What your roll means

Every time you vote, the game rolls a twenty-sided die for your hero. High is good. Low is trouble.

When a choice is risky, your roll plus your hero's skill has to reach the choice's number. Reach it and it works.

The screen in your hand

After everyone has voted, the game shows each hero's die while the dungeon master writes what happens. Three things are on it.

The die was rolled the moment you voted. Changing your vote keeps the same roll, so there is no fishing for a better one. A player who never votes still gets a roll, made for them when the scene resolves.

The number alone is not the whole result. It is the raw die, before your hero's skill is added, and what it is used for depends on the choice the party went with.

The dungeon master weighs your fates…

First the vote, then the dice

The party goes with the choice that got the most votes. The dice only break a tie: when two choices have the same number of votes, the one whose voters rolled the highest die wins.

After that, each die belongs to its own hero. Yours says how your hero fares in what the party chose, even if you voted for something else.

What the choice asks of the dice

Under each choice the game prints what is at stake. That label decides what your die is for. There are four cases, and the game writes out every sum in the party chat so you can check it.

no label

A story choice

Nothing is tested and nothing can fail outright. The dungeon master reads each die as how well that hero's part went: 15 or more goes with a flourish, 5 or less brings trouble, and a 20 or a 1 always gets its moment in the telling.

Athletics check, DC 12

A check

Each hero adds their skill to their own die and tries to reach the number. The party succeeds when at least half its heroes do. If the label says “one hero with help”, only the hero best at it rolls, and anyone trained in that skill helps them.

Bryn: Athletics 14 + 5 = 19 vs DC 12, success
Dexterity save, DC 13

A saving throw

Something is happening to you: a blast, a collapsing roof. Each hero adds their saving throw to their die. Fall short and you take all of the damage; reach the number and you take half.

Mirel: Dexterity save 7 + 2 = 9 vs DC 13, failure
Damage 8 fire: Mirel takes 8, Bryn takes 4
Fight: 2 Goblin Warriors

A fight

From here each round is a vote of its own, and your vote is your own hero's action. Attack or cast and your die plus your attack bonus must reach the foe's Armor Class. Dodge and you make no attack, but attacks against you are rolled twice and the worse die counts.

Bryn attacks Goblin Warrior 1 with Greatsword: 14 (total 18) vs AC 15, hit for 7.

A parley is a check as well: Persuasion, rolled by the party's best talker while the others help. Other labels need no roll from you: a rest, a door to go through, something to buy. A trap is spotted by your hero's passive Perception, which is 10 plus their Perception skill with no die at all. Spells that make the target save (Burning Hands, Sacred Flame) are rolled by the target, not by you. Light matters too: in the dark, a hero with no light and no darkvision fails any Perception or Investigation check, and in a fight they roll two dice to attack and keep the worse.

Where your hero's skill comes from

The number added to your die has two parts.

  • The ability behind it. Each of your hero's six ability scores gives a modifier: half the score above 10, rounded down. Athletics uses Strength, Stealth uses Dexterity, Arcana uses Intelligence, and so on.
  • Training. If your hero is trained in the skill, the save or the weapon, they add their proficiency bonus: +2 at levels 1 to 4, rising to +6 by level 17. Expertise in a skill doubles it, and the right tool for the job adds it too.

So a level 1 fighter with Strength 16, trained in Athletics, rolls Athletics at +5. Each level of exhaustion takes 2 off every check and save.

You never have to add this up. Your hero's sheet in the app lists every skill, save and attack with its total.

How hard is hard

The dungeon master picks the number from a short ladder, by how hard the thing would be for anyone. Your hero's skill then decides how much of the die is enough.

Every point of skill is one more face of the die that works, which is five more chances in a hundred. Past a certain point no die is enough: on a check or a save, a 20 is just a 20.

Two dice instead of one

Sometimes the odds lean. The game then rolls a second die next to the one from your vote and keeps one of the two.

  • Advantage: a friend trained in the skill is helping, or the dungeon master judged that things favour you.
  • Disadvantage: things are against you. In a fight, a foe attacking a hero who dodged rolls this way.

If you have both at once they cancel, and you roll one die as usual. The chat shows the die that was dropped.

Dice the game rolls without you

Your vote's die is the only one you bring. The rules engine rolls the rest, and shows its work in the chat.

  • Who goes first in a fight: a die plus each fighter's Dexterity modifier.
  • Damage: the dice on your weapon or spell, such as 2d6+3 (two six-sided dice plus 3).
  • The monsters' rolls: their attacks against your Armor Class, and their saves against your spells.
  • How monsters take to you: two six-sided dice when you walk into their room. On 5 or less they attack. On 6 or more they watch and wait, and a parley can win them over. The undead, constructs and oozes always attack.
  • Wandering monsters: in a dungeon, one six-sided die every half hour of the adventure's clock, and one at once after a noisy fight or a sprung trap. A 1 brings them (a 1 or a 2 after noise).
  • Death saves for a hero at 0 hit points: 10 or more is a success, three successes and they are stable, three failures and they are dead. After that only a Revivify spell or a temple's price brings them back.
  • Rests and nerve: the hit dice you spend to heal on a short rest, and whether shaken foes stand, flee or surrender.

Where to look in the app

  • Before you vote: the label under each choice says what it will ask of the dice.
  • After the scene resolves: the party chat carries every roll the engine made, sum by sum.
  • Any time: your hero's sheet shows the skill, save and attack numbers that get added to your die.

Adventures begun before the rules engine arrived have no labels. In those, every choice is a story choice and the dice only colour the telling.
