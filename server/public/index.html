How a night goes

Four of you on the couch. One door.

It plays like a tabletop session with a dungeon master, except the dungeon master never needs a night off.

  1. Forge a hero

    Choose a race, a class and a temperament, then describe your hero in your own words. The game embellishes the description and paints the portrait.

    “Copper braid, burn scar on one cheek, patched green cloak, too many rings.”What the player typed for Ilsa Thornwood, elf ranger

  2. Gather the party

    Open a door and share its six-letter code. Up to six friends join from their own phones, pick who will run the game, and talk it over in party chat.

    7JPFDS

  3. The master sets the scene

    A painting of the moment with your heroes in it, a few paragraphs of story, and three ways forward.

  4. Everyone chooses, the dice roll

    Each player votes for themselves and rolls a d20. The dungeon master weighs every vote and every roll, not just the majority. A dissenter still acts. A natural 1 still hurts.

Choose who tells the tale

Four dungeon masters, four very different nights

The Chronicler

A classic tale of heroes, told true.

Fair, sweeping high fantasy: lost kingdoms, ancient magic, honest danger and rewards earned.

The Trickster

Nothing is what it seems. Especially the door.

Talking hats, riddling goblins and staircases that change their minds. The fey keep their word, to the letter.

The Grim Warden

Few return. Fewer return unchanged.

Dark, gothic dread and hard choices, where every lantern matters and victories are bought dearly.

The Bard

Swords, songs and spectacular entrances!

Rooftop duels, pirate ships and villains with magnificent moustaches. The bolder the plan, the louder the applause.

On the mountain

Touch the dragon, if you dare

The home screen belongs to him. He flies in, lands on his summit and watches. Touch him and he answers: sometimes with words, sometimes with fire.

“I have watched a thousand heroes walk through that door. Few walked back.”
